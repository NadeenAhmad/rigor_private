## Hard questions (backup slides B5–B6)

**Q: The Judge-LLM is the same model as the generator. Isn't that self-evaluation?**  
A: Inside the loop, yes: the same model generates and judges, so RIGOR needs no second model. Two things do not depend on it. The rule-based graph checks are deterministic. And the evaluation used a different model family, GPT-5.4, plus ten human experts, who ranked RIGOR first in 70% of cases.

**Q: Is the Non-Iterative baseline a strawman?**  
A: It receives the same retrieved context, but its prompt asks for less (labels and comments). Its biggest failure is dropping columns, down to 6%, which RIGOR prevents by construction with the seed. A prompt-matched ablation is a fair next experiment.

**Q: Could the LLM judge have position bias?**  
A: Fragments were anonymized, but the order was fixed (paper Fig. 7), so we do not rely on the LLM judge alone. The human study points the same way: 28 of 40 first places, with significant agreement. Future runs should randomize the order.

**Q: Why do critical P19 pitfalls remain (multiple domains or ranges)?**  
A: They appear in 3 of 9 runs. A likely source is the union merge: when a property such as a patient link is reused across tables, each table adds its own domain. Merge-time checks and validator feedback inside the loop are the planned fix.

**Q: Why only schemas and documentation, not the data?**  
A: Portability and privacy. Clinical data often cannot leave the hospital, while schemas and documentation can. Instance-level signals, computed as aggregates, are our next step.

**Q: Column coverage drops to 0.77. Why?**  
A: That is DeepSeek on eICU. The seed is complete by construction, but enrichment can drop columns, and coverage is an embedding-based proxy. Stronger per-fragment coverage checks are planned.

**Q: Could the LLMs have seen these databases in training?**  
A: eICU and Chinook are public. The liver-cancer registry is private, so no model saw it, and the LLM judge still ranked RIGOR first on all 16 of its tables.

**Q: Are the external ontologies actually used?**  
A: Retrieved external concepts are passed as context, and alignment axioms are added only on a good match. In the ablation, full RIGOR has the highest external-ontology alignment (F1 0.059 vs 0.033 without retrieval), though absolute values are small.

**Q: How does the traversal handle self-referencing foreign keys (Chinook Employee.ReportsTo)?**  
A: VERIFY IN THE CODE BEFORE THE TALK. Expected answer: a self-reference does not wait on another table, so it does not affect the order; it becomes an object property from the class to itself, which the prompt allows for meaningful recursive relations.

**Q: Why not compare with Gemini 2.5 Pro on BURR?**  
A: In BURR it is a general-purpose LLM prompted to write the mapping directly: a mapping generator, not an ontology-learning system. Against it, RIGOR's class F1 is higher (.73 vs .65), relations are on par (.67 vs .68), attributes lower (.52 vs .56), and RIGOR also produces labels, hierarchy and provenance.
